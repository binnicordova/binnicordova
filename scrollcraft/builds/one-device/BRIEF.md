## 1. Vibe, in three to five words

> "minimalist as the iphone duo page" — Binni, verbatim

Read as: Apple product-page minimalism. Pure black ground, one object lit in the
centre of the frame, type at two extremes of scale and nothing in between,
motion that is precise rather than abundant.

References `[authored]`, none of them websites:
- The physical unboxing of a phone: black, matte, one object, no noise.
- An exploded-view technical drawing in a service manual.
- A dark room with one practical light and a machined object on a turntable.

## 2. The scroll journey, section by section `[authored]`

A single phone in black space, and the camera never cuts away from it.

1. The phone is far away and rotating. You read who this is.
2. The camera closes on the glass. A real shipped app is running on it.
3. The camera crosses the glass and the app comes apart into the engineering
   layers underneath it, one at a time, while the UI keeps running on top.
4. The camera reverses back out through the stack, and the record is there in
   the same space: the companies, the real numbers.
5. The space widens and the rest of the shipped work is arrayed in depth.
6. Everything settles. One phone, at rest, with a way to reach him.

## 3. The energy curve `[authored]`

Quiet, quiet, loud, settling, quiet, still. The loud part is one act and it is
act three. Acts one and two deliberately under-deliver so that crossing the
glass has somewhere to arrive from. Act six is the quietest thing on the page,
quieter than act one.

## 4. The feeling curve, and the peak `[authored]`

One line per act: the emotion, then what on screen causes it.

| Act | Feeling | What causes it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · Signal | **Anticipation.** Stillness with something in it. | A real shipped product, lit and turning, far off in a black volume. Nothing else on screen but a name. |
| 2 · Surface | **Recognition.** "That is a real product." | The camera arrives at the glass and an actual shipped app is running under your scroll, not a mockup of one. |
| 3 · Inside | **Awe.** | You pass through the glass and the app delaminates: the UI keeps running on the front plane while the bridge, the native modules, the offline store and the cloud separate behind it, each one labelled with the real technology. |

| 6 · Arrival | **Invitation. Calm.** | Everything stops. One phone at rest, his face in the same light, one address. |

No two adjacent acts carry the same feeling.

**The peak is act 3.** Written as the sentence a visitor says to a friend:

> "It's the site where you scroll *into* the phone and the app comes apart into
> the stack it's actually built from, while it keeps running."

It gets 3.2 viewport-heights, against 1.8 for the next longest act, and act 2
before it is deliberately the quietest of the first half.

**Authored silence:** the last 0.57vh of act 1 (the hero copy closes at 0.93vh

## 6. One thing no site they have seen does `[authored]`

Engineering portfolios show you the screen or they show you an architecture
diagram. They never show you that the screen *is* the top of the diagram. The
delamination does exactly that, in one continuous move, with the UI still live.

## 7. How far from premium-minimal

Answered: Apple product-page minimal. That governs everything. Two type scales,
one accent, no cards, no gradients on text, no glow, no eyebrows.

## 8. One unbroken world, or distinct scenes?

Answered: **one unbroken world.** Continuous-world grammar, worldflight mode.
One fixed stage, one spacer, nothing else in document flow. The world is a black
volume containing one phone; the camera is a continuous function of scroll and
never cuts. The screen's *content* changes between legs, which is a phone doing
what a phone does, not a cut in the world.

## 12b. The device

iPhone 18 Pro, built in CSS from the description on Apple's own page rather
than from their assets: unibody with Ceramic Shield on both sides, the
redesigned Dynamic Island, the burgundy of this generation, the full-width
camera plateau, Action Button and Camera Control on the rails.

**Apple's renders and their `#battery-life` footage are theirs and are not
republished here.** What was taken from that section is its *technique*, which
is the same one this engine already uses: a short clip, scrubbed by scroll,
the device large in frame with the screen content as the subject and the copy
beside it. Landscape there, portrait here, because these are phone apps.

The body has real thickness: a back face and two rails rotated a quarter turn
into depth off the body's own edges. At thirteen degrees of yaw a rail projects
to about seven pixels, which is physically correct and visually nothing, so
what carries it is the specular: one bright band down the rail and a bright
hairline chamfer where it turns into the glass. That is how a product shot
lights a phone, and it is why the front reads as the face of a solid rather
than a sheet of paper.

The screen canvas is 1206x2622 reduced, the real capture aspect of the device
his own recordings came off, so those need no cropping at runtime at all.

## 12c. The photograph

The first version floated the bare cutout and faded its bottom edge out with a
mask. Against a page that has a device and a field behind it, that fade reads
as the photograph having been cut off, which is what he said it looked like.

He is a plate now. The subject is lifted with macOS Vision, the alpha eroded
two pixels to kill the violet rim the original backdrop left in his hair and
shoulder, and composited onto its own ground: a burgundy pool of light that
ties to the device, both shoulders in frame, anchored to the bottom edge. The
bottom of the image is the bottom of an object, and the page rounds its corners
in CSS. He arrives as one more plate in a room already full of them, which is
the one that happens to be a person.

No image model was used. There is no key configured and none was needed: the
new asset is a real composite of his own photograph, built by
`portrait.sh`, and it can be rebuilt from source at any time.

## 13. What is shown, and what was cut

These are real production recordings and they carry real data. What happens to
each, and why.

**miMarket is shown unblurred, at Binni's instruction.** The first cut blurred
its content band, and he said plainly: "Dont blur the content apps, fix it."
He is right that it looked broken, and it is his call: he built the app, he
made the recording, and he is the one who answers for it.

So the blur is gone and the window moved to the client-detail screen, which is
the only part of that recording that shows what the app actually does. What is
now legible in `assets/1-cocacola.mp4`: one customer's business record. A shop
name, a client number, a Chilean RUT and a street address. Business records
rather than personal ones, about four pixels tall as rendered on the page, and
plainly readable in the downloaded file.

The alternative, if that is ever not wanted, is one number in `encode.sh`: the
window `16.0 / 6.2` is the Cold Equipment screen and carries no customer data
at all. It is nearly static and it shows a React Navigation dev warning, which
is why it is not the default.

Everything else is a choice of window rather than a change to the pixels:

- **Itaú** is clean for 2.9 seconds only. At 3.2s it types a personal email
  into a form, and it ends on an error screen. The leg uses the login and
  consent screens and is slowed to fill its span.
- **Platanitos** stops before the account screen, which shows a full legal
  name and an email address.
- **cocap**'s photo picker holds personal family photographs including
  children. The leg uses the onboarding carousel and the event form, and stops
  before the picker opens.
- **PlacaOk**'s share sheet exposes a phone number; only an early frame is used
  as a field plate.
- **PickPointer** is a still only, taken before the Google sign-in screen that
  shows a full legal name and an email.

## 15. Motion off

Reduced motion is not a degraded copy of the page, it is the same page held
still, and it gets its own composition on each viewport. No clip is ever
fetched; the posters are the film.

- **Desktop.** The camera parks at a three-quarter angle, the stack is shown
  already exploded so the idea of the page survives without the scroll that
  reveals it, and the portrait sits clear to the right of the device. An overlap
  reads as depth while things are moving and as a collision when they are not.
- **Phone.** Its own block: the object shrinks, the whole scene lifts clear of
  the copy band, the two deepest plates in the field are dropped rather than
  shrunk into noise, and the callout labels are hidden because six of them
  cannot be read beside a plane that is already 62vw wide.

Verified by screenshot at three positions on both viewports, and mechanically by
`verify/checks.mjs`.
